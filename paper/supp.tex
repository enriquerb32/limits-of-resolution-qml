\section{Data, Labels and Features}
\label{app:pipeline}

\subsection{Monthly Panel and Labels}

The daily files of each station (precipitation; maximum and minimum pressure; maximum and minimum temperature; sunshine duration; mean wind speed and maximum gust) are read separately and placed on a full daily calendar from the first to the last date found in any of them (\path{code/etl_v2.py}). Precipitation is taken from its own file only: a trace (``Ip'') counts as 0\,mm, and an empty or unparseable entry is missing. At the daily level, covariates stay missing where they are missing; no value is carried forward or backward. \Cref{tab:etl} lists the spans and the shares of missing days.

The four stations, their test windows and their test-set class prevalence are in \Cref{tab:station_coverage}.

Monthly aggregation (\path{code/local_experiments/dataset_v2.py}) produces, per station and calendar month: the \emph{label total}, the sum of daily precipitation, defined when at most 3 days of the month are missing; the \emph{precipitation lag}, the sum over observed days rescaled to the month length, defined with at least 10 observed days; and the covariates, means over observed days (sunshine and ET$_0$ sums as means times the month length), defined with at least 10 observed days, with at most two consecutive missing months interpolated linearly within the station (in a longer gap, its first two months; \Cref{sec:lookahead}). ET$_0$ follows FAO-56 Penman--Monteith with $u_2=0.748\,u_{10}$ from the observed mean wind (FAO-56 eq.~47 for a 10\,m anemometer). Every calendar month is kept, and a window with target month $t$ is used only if $t$ has a label total, month $t-1$ has all covariates and the 12 lags $t-12,\dots,t-1$ are defined. Training-partition medians replace undefined cells before the min--max scalers are fitted, so that rows that never enter a window do not stretch the scales. Thresholds ($P_{60}=29.9$\,mm, $P_{90}$) are percentiles of all 2{,}847 labelled months up to 1996 of the four stations (Alicante 699, Barcelona 809, San Javier 629, Valencia 710), not only of the months that are window targets; the precipitation records start before the first training window (Barcelona March 1924, Valencia November 1937, Alicante October 1938, San Javier August 1944), so the Barcelona count exceeds the 708 months of 1938--1996.

\subsection{Lookahead Audit}
\label{sec:lookahead}

A forecast for month $t$ is issued at the end of month $t-1$, so its inputs may use only data up to that date. Three steps of the monthly pipeline used for the results of the paper do not satisfy this. (i) The interpolation of missing monthly covariates is two-sided: a missing $t-1$ is filled from $t-2$ and $t$, and the first two months of a longer gap from the month after the gap, up to 18 months later. (ii) The antecedent-summer evapotranspiration (\texttt{et0\_verano\_prev}) of a summer with an incomplete June--August record is interpolated towards the next complete summer and back-filled. (iii) A missing one-month evapotranspiration lag is replaced by the station mean over the full record, test years included. The chronological split does not prevent any of these.

\textbf{Truncation test} (\path{exp47_truncation_audit.py}). For each test target month $t$ the monthly panel was rebuilt from the daily file cut at the end of $t-1$, with the transformations fitted on the training partition (anomaly statistics, medians, scalers and $P_{60}$, all from data up to 1996 and therefore unaffected by the cut); the label of $t$ is the outcome and comes from the full record. For all 529 test windows the 35 inputs and the eligibility of the window are identical to those of the full pipeline, and no window becomes eligible only after the cut. The reason is that the gaps that the interpolation fills lie before the test period or at stations whose records stop: 54 station-months receive an interpolated covariate; 26 of them are the last antecedent month of an eligible window (24 training, 2 validation windows) and none of a test window. The incomplete summers after 2011 (San Javier) and 2012 (Valencia) have no later complete summer to interpolate from. The daily file does not record when each value became available, so this is a retrospective check of lookahead in the processing, not a reconstruction of what could have been issued in real time.

\textbf{Causal pipeline.} With \texttt{V2\_CAUSAL=1}, a missing covariate is carried forward from the last observed month for at most two months, \texttt{et0\_verano\_prev} is the last complete summer (before the first one, the station's training median), and a missing evapotranspiration lag takes the station's training mean. The same truncation test gives no change for any test window. The causal panel has 533 test windows instead of 529, because carrying forward also fills the last months before the covariate records of Valencia and San Javier stop. \Cref{tab:lookahead} compares models retrained on the two panels on the 529 common test windows: the differences are an order of magnitude below the standard error of one AUC (0.024) and below every minimum detectable difference between complete models. Small as they are, these changes act on differences that lie near the resolution limit. \Cref{tab:causal} repeats the two primary families on the causal refits: every comparison that is not resolved on the archived pipeline stays unresolved, but Random Forest against the LSTM is no longer resolved, and Random Forest against \texttt{LSTM+QNN} becomes resolved after the Holm correction. With clusters of two and three consecutive years its causal $p$-values are 0.020 and 0.076 on the common rows (archived 0.051 and 0.131), and those of Random Forest against the LSTM 0.058 and 0.143. Both verdicts therefore depend on the fitting pipeline as well as on the cluster definition, which is why the main text treats them as exploratory. The archived pipeline remains the primary analysis: every model, control and reference of the paper shares it, it was the primary analysis before the causal refits were made, and only the five models of the primary families were refitted.

\textbf{Label completeness.} The label rule admits up to 3 missing days, and with missing days the observed total is a lower bound of the true one, so that a fixed threshold can only produce false negatives. In this panel all 529 test targets, all 528 labelled validation months and all 2{,}847 labelled months up to 1996 that define $P_{60}$ have complete records; one labelled month of the test period has missing days but is not a window target. A sensitivity analysis restricted to complete months therefore reproduces the reported AUCs and $P_{60}$ exactly.

\subsection{Engineered Features}
\label{sec:glossary}

Each window has 35 covariates: 12 precipitation lags, 17 physical and synoptic indicators of the last antecedent month (\Cref{tab:glossary}), 2 cyclic terms of the target month and 4 station indicators. Anomalies and scaling statistics are computed from the training years (1938--1996) only. The covariates are candidate predictors, not physical state variables of a numerical weather prediction model.

\subsection{Specification of the Experiments}
\label{app:architectures}

\Cref{tab:core_spec} specifies every model of the 15-seed experiments: architecture, optimizer, training budget and number of trainable parameters. All models are fitted on the same rows, with the seeds set immediately before each model is built.

\textbf{Circuit details.} In \texttt{LSTM+QNN} each of the two layers re-uploads the angles as $R_Z(q_j)R_Y(q_j)$ ($R_Y$ acts first on $|0\rangle$, so $R_Z$ is not a global phase) before one \texttt{StronglyEntanglingLayers} block of range $r=1$, a ring of nearest-neighbour CNOTs (the template is called once per layer, so both layers have $r=1$); the readout is $\langle Z_j\rangle$ on every qubit, and gradients are computed by backpropagation through the \texttt{default.qubit} state-vector simulator in double precision. Because LayerNorm standardizes the four readouts of each row, the model is approximately invariant to a common contraction of the $\langle Z_j\rangle$, such as global depolarizing noise would cause; the invariance is not exact, because of the $\epsilon$ in the LayerNorm denominator, it breaks down when the contraction makes the spread of the readouts comparable to $\sqrt{\epsilon}$, and it does not extend to shot noise or to qubit-dependent noise. \texttt{LSTM+QTL} feeds a trainable adapter ($67\to8\to4$, angles in $[0,\pi]$) into a 2-layer circuit with a single $R_Y$ encoding (per-coordinate frequencies $\{0,\pm1\}$; one \texttt{StronglyEntanglingLayers} call with two layers, so PennyLane's default ranges $r=1$ and $r=2$). The separable Fourier controls have 36--132 parameters in the map, \texttt{Fourier-4} covering the circuit's full per-coordinate spectrum without its cross-coordinate products; a full degree-4 tensor basis would have $9^4=6{,}561$ features for 2{,}332 training rows. All controls share the training loop of the model they are compared with (\Cref{tab:core_spec}). The tree ensembles were tuned with Optuna (TPE sampler): Random Forest selected 350 trees of depth 11 (minimum split 13, minimum leaf 10, $\sqrt{p}$ features), and XGBoost a learning rate of 0.036 with trees of depth 3 (median 16 trees after early stopping, 2--3 in five seeds).

\subsection{Estimators}
\label{app:noise_budget}


\textbf{Estimators.} For two models scored on the same 529 rows, the DeLong estimator gives the covariance of the two AUCs from their structural components \citep{delong1988}; it treats station-months as independent. The block bootstrap resamples the 19 calendar years (2008--2026) with replacement, keeping all stations of a year together, recomputes both AUCs on each resample ($B=2{,}000$) and takes percentile intervals \citep{field2007}; the moving-block variant with blocks of several consecutive years \citep{kunsch1989} is a sensitivity check. The two-way bootstrap draws a calendar-year resample and a resample of the 15 seeds, one draw shared by both models when they share the fitted LSTM of a seed and one per model otherwise, and recomputes the difference of seed-averaged AUCs. The cluster-robust estimator uses the influence values $\psi_i=(\bar v^{A}_{10,i}-\bar v^{B}_{10,i}-\hat\Delta)/n_1$ for positives and $(\bar v^{A}_{01,j}-\bar v^{B}_{01,j}-\hat\Delta)/n_0$ for negatives, where $\bar v$ are the DeLong components averaged over seeds; $\sum_i\psi_i^2$ equals the DeLong variance of $\hat\Delta$ up to factors $(n-1)/n$, and the year sums of $\psi$ give the cluster-robust one. Writing the year sum as $S_u=\sum_s S_{u,s}$ over stations $s$, $\sum_u S_u^2=\sum_{u,s}S_{u,s}^2+\sum_u\sum_{s\ne t}S_{u,s}S_{u,t}$ separates the within-station and cross-station terms; the permutation null permutes the year labels independently within each station (2{,}000 permutations).

\textbf{Seed-mean statistic.} The AUC of one model is the mean of the DeLong structural components over positives and over negatives, so the mean of the 15 AUCs equals the AUC statistic built from components averaged over seeds, and the paired DeLong variance of $\hat\Delta$ follows from the averaged components. This treats the 15 seeds as fixed and so conditions on the fitted models; the two-way bootstrap relaxes this by resampling seeds.

\subsection{Cross-Station Structure of the Cluster Variance}
\label{app:cancellation}

\Cref{tab:cancellation} decomposes the calendar-year cluster variance of four paired differences (\path{exp37_cancellation.py}). The cross-station term is the sum over years of the products of the contributions of different stations; it is negative when, within a year, a model gains at one station and loses at another. The permutation null permutes the years independently within each station (2{,}000 permutations), which removes the co-movement of stations and keeps the within-station structure. The last two columns recompute the ratio after removing, from the influence values, the mean of each station (and class) or of each station$\times$calendar month$\times$class cell.

A cluster standard error below the DeLong one means that the advantage of one model is steadier from year to year than independent months would suggest. In the years in which Random Forest gains on the LSTM at one station, it tends to lose at another, so the station contributions partly cancel within each year. Shuffling the years independently within each station, which breaks that link, brings the ratio back towards 0.75--0.87, and the observed values lie in the lower tail of that distribution ($p=0.07$ and 0.12; \Cref{tab:cancellation}). The cancellation comes from seasonal patterns that differ between stations and between the two model families, and it did not appear on the second panel (\Cref{M-sec:replication}).

\textbf{The cancellation by period} (\path{exp45_cluster_periods.py}). Restricted to 2008--2012, the only five years with more than two stations (all four in 2008--2011), the cross-station term is $-88$\,\% and $-99$\,\% of the within-station one for RF $-$ LSTM and RF $-$ \texttt{LSTM+QNN}, and Random Forest has no advantage (gaps $-0.004$ and $-0.0005$); in 2013--2026, with two stations, the terms are $-25$\,\% and $-3$\,\%, the cluster/iid ratios 0.65 and 0.87, and the gaps $+0.023$ and $+0.030$. Because the composition of the test set changes with the period, a difference in the relative behaviour of the models between the two periods is an alternative reading of the same pattern, and the two cannot be separated with five years of more than two stations. Removing a constant per station changes nothing, whereas removing the mean of each station, calendar month and class brings the ratio to 1.0--1.06. Removing the partial year 2026 (nine rows, January--May) leaves the ratios unchanged (0.60 and 0.73). The effect was stronger in the earlier version of the analysis, whose labels were partly wrong (\Cref{app:v1}).

\subsection{Simulation of Within-Year Cancellation}
\label{app:cancellation_sim}

The observed ratios of 0.59 and 0.72 (\Cref{tab:cancellation}) are rare in the calibration of \Cref{app:fewclusters}. \path{exp61_cancellation_calibration.py} asks whether a mechanism of cancellation between stations would make them common, and how the intervals behave when the data show such a ratio. By the design effect, a ratio $r$ over clusters of $m$ rows implies an intraclass correlation $(r^2-1)/(m-1)$ of the influence values; with $m=27.8$ rows per year the observed ratios imply $-0.024$ and $-0.018$, against a lower bound of $-1/(m-1)=-0.037$.

\textbf{Search.} On the real test mask, 1{,}000 replications per cell, thirteen mechanisms that make station contributions cancel within a year (year$\times$station effects with opposite loadings up to $\tau_c=1$, prevalence dipoles with a scorer that uses the base rate, station effects centred within each year, and a swap of the advantage between the two station groups) give median ratios of 1.05--2.45: random within-year effects add variance to the yearly sums, whatever their signs across stations, and the probability of a ratio $\le0.72$ is at most 0.033. Only a pattern that recurs every year can lower the ratio legitimately, because every year holds every month and the years become balanced blocks; a fixed station$\times$month pattern of the scores or of the signal of one scorer lowers the median ratio to 0.83--0.97.

\textbf{Coverage given a low ratio.} \Cref{tab:cancellation_sim} gives, in ten mechanisms and 20{,}000 replications each, the coverage of the 95\,\% intervals for the difference for new years, over all replications and over those whose cluster/iid ratio is as low as observed. CR2 is calibrated overall (0.947--0.955 over both true differences) but covers 0.78--0.90 when the ratio is $\le0.72$ and 0.70--0.86 when it is $\le0.59$: a low ratio mostly reflects an underestimated cluster variance. Flooring the CR2 standard error at the iid one keeps the coverage at 0.92--0.99 in those replications and makes the interval conservative overall (0.96--0.99). Applied to the contrasts of the paper (\path{exp62_floored_cr2_observed.py}), the floor changes only the two exploratory verdicts, Random Forest against the LSTM ($p$ from 0.041 to 0.204) and against \texttt{LSTM+QNN} (0.031 to 0.107); the frozen circuits and Random Forest against \texttt{LSTM+QTL} remain resolved. The within-station structure of the real data (\Cref{tab:cancellation}, permutation null centred at 0.75--0.92) is not fully reproduced by these mechanisms, so the floor is a conservative rule, not a proof that the two differences are absent.

\textbf{A trigger for the floor} (\path{exp63_floor_threshold.py}). Item 1 of the protocol floors the CR2 standard error only when the cluster/iid ratio (CR1 over DeLong) is at or below the 5th percentile of its distribution on the real test mask with independent cases: 0.708 in 20{,}000 replications (median 0.975; 1st and 10th percentiles 0.61 and 0.76). In the ten mechanisms of \Cref{tab:cancellation_sim} (5{,}000 replications per mechanism and true difference, 0 and 0.02) the 5th percentile of the ratio is 0.61--0.84 and the rule triggers in 0.9--19.6\,\% of replications, most often under the seasonal patterns that lower the ratio legitimately. The rule covers at 0.95--0.98 overall, against 0.95--0.96 for CR2 and 0.96--0.99 for the floor applied to every replication, and at 0.89--0.99 among the replications that trigger it (45 to 981 per cell), where CR2 covers at 0.73--0.89. In this paper the unfloored CR2 interval, fixed before the rule existed, decides, and the rule is a sensitivity check. On the contrasts of the paper it triggers only for Random Forest against the LSTM (ratio 0.594), which with the floor would not be resolved ($p=0.204$), with the MDE of the floored procedure, 0.033 (\Cref{tab:mde_ci}); Random Forest against \texttt{LSTM+QNN} (0.723) and against \texttt{LSTM+QTL} (0.779) lie above it, so their CR2 verdicts stand. The trigger was set after the primary analysis and from this panel, so it is post hoc, and it is a property of this design: another test set needs its own calibration; it was fixed as a percentile of the independent-cases distribution before the contrasts were sorted against it, and Random Forest against \texttt{LSTM+QNN} falls 0.014 above it. A trigger of 0.75 would also floor Random Forest against \texttt{LSTM+QNN} ($p=0.107$; already exploratory for the reasons of \Cref{M-sec:budget}) and Random Forest against XGBoost ($p$ from 0.110 to 0.224; not resolved either way), and change no other AUC verdict. On the Brier contrasts of \Cref{tab:brier_inference}, for which the trigger was not calibrated (a Brier-specific trigger would be computed in the same way, as the 5th percentile of the Brier cluster/iid ratio simulated on the design with independent cases), it fires for two recalibrated contrasts and changes one verdict: the LSTM against the climatology (ratio 0.66; $p$ from 0.016 to 0.095).

\subsection{Uncertainty of the MDE and Equivalence Tests}
\label{app:mde_ci}

\textbf{Uncertainty of the MDE} (\path{exp57_mde_ci_equivalence.py}). The MDE of the primary procedure, $(t_{0.975,\nu}+t_{0.80,\nu})\,\text{SE}_{\text{CR2}}$, treats the CR2 standard error as known. \Cref{tab:mde_ci} gives two 95\,\% intervals for it. The first uses the Satterthwaite approximation that defines the Bell--McCaffrey degrees of freedom, $\nu\,\text{SE}^2/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_\nu$, so that $\sigma$ lies in $\text{SE}\,[\{\nu/\chi^2_{0.975,\nu}\}^{1/2},\{\nu/\chi^2_{0.025,\nu}\}^{1/2}]$; with $\nu=15.9$ the interval is 0.74--1.52 times the point MDE for every contrast. The second is a percentile bootstrap over the 19 calendar years ($B=2{,}000$; years drawn with replacement and relabelled, $\text{SE}_{\text{CR2}}$ and $\nu$ recomputed), 0.45--1.44 times the point MDE. If the true standard error lay at either end of the first interval, the power of the design at the reported MDE would be 0.44 or 0.96.

\subsection{Inference on the Brier Score}
\label{app:brier}

The inference of the main text applies to any metric with per-case influence values. For a difference of seed-mean Brier scores the influence value of a case is its centred loss difference divided by $N$, so the CR2 leverage correction of a mean is exact (\path{qml_resolution.cluster_robust_brier_delta}; \path{exp58_brier_inference.py}). \Cref{tab:brier_inference} repeats the two primary families, the Fourier controls and the comparisons with the climatology, as trained and after a per-seed Platt map fitted on the validation partition (the refit of the right block of \Cref{tab:brier}, the only archive with validation predictions of these models). Both columns reproduce the Brier scores of \Cref{tab:brier}.

As trained, the Brier score resolves most differences, among them Random Forest against the three neural models and \texttt{LSTM+QNN} against its Linear-4D control (worse by 0.0046), all after Holm. These differences mostly measure calibration: every model except the climatology was trained with positive-class weighting, and the as-trained calibration errors are 0.10--0.14 (\Cref{tab:brier}). After recalibration no difference of the primary families passes Holm, \texttt{LSTM+QNN} is not distinguished from Linear-4D ($+0.0012$, $p=0.44$), and Random Forest against \texttt{LSTM+QNN} and the LSTM against \texttt{LSTM+QNN} are resolved before correction only ($p=0.027$). The standalone LSTM, Linear-4D and \texttt{LSTM+QNN} remain worse than the recalibrated climatology (CR2 $p=0.010$--0.028; secondary, unadjusted); with the floor of protocol item 1, which the LSTM triggers (ratio 0.66), the LSTM is not ($p=0.095$; \Cref{app:cancellation_sim}). The Brier MDEs of 15-seed means are 0.004--0.010 after recalibration. No verdict of the main text changes: none of the AUC statements concerns calibration, and on the recalibrated Brier score, as on the AUC, no learned model is better than the climatology and the circuit shows no advantage over its interface controls.

\subsection{Simulation: All Scenarios}
\label{app:sim_all}

\textbf{Earlier simulation: DeLong and the block bootstrap.} This simulation compares only DeLong and the percentile year-block bootstrap; the calibration that selects the primary procedure, with every candidate on the real coverage mask, is in \Cref{M-sec:simulation} and \Cref{app:fewclusters}. Two binormal scorers with noise correlation $\rho=0.9$, which reproduces the paired DeLong standard error of the classical comparisons (0.011), have design AUCs of 0.65 and $0.65+\Delta$ without year effects. Labels have prevalence 0.41 and 528 rows are split among 19 years, the size of the test set of the first version of the analysis (\Cref{app:v1}); the version matched to the present test set (529 rows, prevalence 0.406) is in \Cref{app:panel_matched}. Year effects enter as a prevalence shift ($\tau_\pi$), a shift shared by both scorers ($\tau_s$) and a shift specific to each scorer ($\tau_m$): scenario B has $\tau_\pi=\tau_s=0.3$, C adds $\tau_m=0.3$, D is C with unequal year sizes, and E has all three equal to 0.6 (500 replications each).

The simulations (\Cref{tab:sim,fig:sim}) give three results. With year effects shared by the two scorers (B) DeLong is calibrated (type-I error 0.040, coverage 0.960), whereas the block bootstrap rejects the exact null in 7.4\,\% of replications. With scorer-specific year effects the DeLong standard error is too small (ratio of Monte Carlo SD to mean SE 1.07, 1.17 and 1.57 in C--E; type-I error 0.068, 0.096 and 0.220), whereas the block standard error tracks the SD (1.01--1.08) with a type-I error of 0.074--0.084: neither procedure is uniformly better calibrated. Where DeLong is calibrated, power follows the MDE rule: in B (MDE 0.031) a true difference of 0.029 is detected in 74\,\% of replications and 0.0145 in 32\,\%. The panel-matched version gives type-I errors of 0.048--0.058 for DeLong and 0.064--0.088 for the block bootstrap, and only 17--24\,\% power at an observed gap of 0.013. None of these mechanisms produces a cluster standard error well below the DeLong one (\Cref{M-sec:budget}).

\textbf{Mechanism and population AUC.}\label{app:sim_mechanism} In each replication year $u$ receives a prevalence effect $\tau_\pi\eta_u$ on the logit of $\pi=0.41$, and scorer $k$ is $s_k=\mu_k y+e_k+\tau_s w_u+\tau_m v_{k,u}$, with $e_k=\sqrt\rho\,z+\sqrt{1-\rho}\,\varepsilon_k$, $w_u$ shared, $v_{k,u}$ independent between scorers, all standard normal, and $\mu_k=\sqrt2\,\Phi^{-1}(\mathrm{AUC}_k)$. In D the shares of the rows among years follow a Dirichlet(3) draw independent of the year effects; the mean of the estimates then differs from the equal-weight reference by $+0.0002$, $-0.0008$ and $-0.0004$ for design differences of 0, 0.015 and 0.03 (Monte Carlo standard error about 0.0008). The target is the difference for new years, computed as the mean of four independent draws of 400{,}000 rows. Because the year shifts are independent of the prevalence effect, it also has a closed form: in an infinite population of independent years a positive and a negative come from different years with probability 1, so their score difference is normal with mean $\mu_k$ and variance $2(1+\tau_s^2+\tau_m^2)$, and
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{AUC}_k=\Phi\!\left(\frac{\mu_k}{\sqrt{2\,(1+\tau_s^2+\tau_m^2)}}\right),
\end{equation}
the AUC for new independent years, not the expected AUC of a fixed set of 19 years. At a design difference of 0.03 it gives population differences of 0.0290 (B), 0.0280 (C and D) and 0.0238 (E), in agreement with the Monte Carlo references (\path{exp49_review_minor.py}). DeLong tests use the paired estimator; the block bootstrap resamples years ($B=300$ per replication).

\subsection{Frequency Spectrum and Angles of the Trained Circuits}
\label{app:spectrum}

For each of the 15 trained circuits of \texttt{LSTM+QNN}, the exact readouts $\langle Z_j\rangle$ were evaluated on the angles produced for the training and test rows (\path{exp34_circuit_spectrum.py}, part A). For the spectrum, each angle was swept over a full period $[0,2\pi)$ with the other three at the values of a test row, so the shares below describe the function on the whole torus; the least-squares fits that follow use only the angles the model actually visits (roughly 0.4--2.6\,rad, less than half a period), on which higher frequencies are well approximated by degree-1 terms. The two statements are therefore compatible: 24\,\% of the variance at frequency 2 over the full period, and $R^2=0.984$ for the degree-1 tensor basis on the visited range. Split by the per-coordinate frequency of its Fourier components, the variance of the readout over the full period is: 68.2\,\%, 24.2\,\%, 6.7\,\%, 0.9\,\% of the variance lies at frequencies 1, 2, 3 and 4 (circuits with random weights: 59.6\,\%, 29.1\,\%, 9.9\,\%, 1.4\,\%). Least-squares fits on the training angles, evaluated on the test angles, give $R^2$ of 0.984 (degree-1 tensor basis, 81 functions; range 0.95--1.00), 0.987 (degree-2 tensor basis, 625 functions; 0.88--1.00), 0.850 (separable degree 2) and 0.854 (separable degree 4). Fitting the degree-1 tensor basis to circuits with random weights on the same angles gives 0.974 (0.94--1.00): the near-linearity per coordinate is a property of this encoding on the visited angles. For the median seed, the 1st, 50th and 99th percentiles of the test angles of the four qubits are 0.65, 0.37, 1.01 and 0.58; 1.72, 1.40, 1.86 and 1.45; and 2.58, 2.48, 2.62 and 2.29 rad; between 0 and 14\,\% of the test angles of a seed lie within $\pi/18$ of 0 or $\pi$.

\subsection{Emulation and Substitution on Common Angles}
\label{app:emulation}

\path{exp52_emulation_substitution.py} uses the 15 trained \texttt{LSTM+QNN} models of \path{exp31_shot_noise.py} (\path{checkpoints/exp31/}; their test probabilities reproduce the archived ones to $10^{-6}$; mean AUC 0.660 against 0.659 for the core fits). \emph{Emulation}: the four readouts are evaluated on the grid $q_j\in\{2\pi m/9\}$, $m=0,\dots,8$, and a four-dimensional discrete Fourier transform gives the $9^4=6{,}561$ coefficients of each readout; this is exact because the two re-uploading layers bound the frequencies at 4 per angle. Evaluated on the test angles and passed through the trained LayerNorm and output layer, the reconstruction differs from the circuit by at most $2.6\times10^{-14}$ in the logits, and the AUC is identical for every seed. Keeping only frequencies $|k_j|\le1$ (or $\le2$) per angle loses 0.058 (0.011) AUC on average, with a maximum of 0.215 (0.070), and changes the readouts by up to 0.95 (0.64) on uniform angles. \emph{Substitution}: for each seed, the LSTM and projection are frozen in evaluation mode and the angles of the training, validation and test rows computed once; on them the circuit (new random weights), Linear-4D, separable Fourier-2 and the 81- and 625-function tensor maps, each followed by LayerNorm and a linear output, are trained with the loop of the paper (AdamW $5\times10^{-3}$, batch 16, at most 25 epochs, patience 6) and the seed of the frozen model. Every head of a seed shares its extractor, so the years$\times$seeds bootstrap draws seeds jointly. On the common angles of the substitution test (\Cref{tab:common_angles}), the original end-to-end circuit lies 0.008 below the 81-function map (CR2 $p=0.035$; the years$\times$seeds interval reaches zero).

\subsection{Finite-Shot Inference}
\label{app:shots}

\path{exp31_shot_noise.py} refits the 15 seeds of \texttt{LSTM+QNN} (mean exact AUC 0.6599, against 0.6587 for the archived fits) and, for each seed and shot budget, samples the 16-outcome distribution of the circuit jointly 50 times, estimating each $\langle Z_j\rangle$ from the samples (\Cref{tab:shots}). With parameter-shift gradients, each training row and step would need 81 circuit evaluations: one forward pass, 48 for the 24 weights and 32 for the 16 encoding-gate occurrences through which the classical layers are trained; training on hardware was not simulated. The refit also measures re-execution: neural training on CPU is not bit-reproducible across software versions and thread settings, and within the pinned environment refitting the 15 seeds changed their mean AUC by 0.001, a third of the uncertainty of a 15-seed mean and an order of magnitude below the MDE.

\subsection{ECMWF SEAS5}
\label{app:seas5}

Monthly mean total precipitation (\texttt{seasonal-monthly-single-levels}, system 51, members 0--24) was downloaded through the CDS API on 9--10 November 2026 for the hindcast (1981--2016) and forecast (2017--2026) starts of every month and interpolated bilinearly to the stations (\path{exp33_seas5_reference.py}). Three probability definitions were computed; the quantile-corrected one was specified before the analysis and is the only one used in the inferential comparisons: the fraction of members above the model's own quantile at level $1-p_{\text{clim}}$ of the station and calendar month, from the 1981--1996 hindcasts. The ensemble-mean variant maps the standardized ensemble-mean anomaly through a normal distribution; the uncorrected variant counts members above the observed threshold. Platt maps and blends are fitted on the validation partition (1997--2007).

\textbf{Increments.} Adding a model to a combination of SEAS5 and the climatology changes the AUC by $-0.008$ to $-0.001$ at lead 2 and by $-0.003$ to $+0.001$ at lead 1, with no primary interval excluding zero (CR2 $p\ge0.25$ at lead 2 and $p\ge0.62$ at lead 1; DeLong $p\ge0.31$; \Cref{tab:seas5_blends}).

\textbf{Lead 1.} Lead 1 starts on the first day of the target month and is released several days later, so it is a reference issued after the statistical models, not a demonstrated upper bound. SEAS5 then scores AUC 0.728, against 0.685 for the climatology and 0.678 for Random Forest (\Cref{tab:seas5_blends}). Its gain over the climatology, $+0.043$, is not resolved: the primary interval includes zero (CR2 $[-0.008,+0.095]$, $p=0.093$), although DeLong rejects the null ($p=0.034$). Random Forest and \texttt{LSTM+QNN} lie 0.050 and 0.070 below SEAS5; the second difference is resolved (CR2 $p=0.014$), the first is not (CR2 $[-0.102,+0.002]$, $p=0.056$). The dynamical forecast is the only predictor with detectable information beyond the base rates: with the station$\times$month label it scores 0.639, against 0.479 for the climatology ($+0.159$, CR2 $[+0.081,+0.237]$). Adding a statistical model to SEAS5 and the climatology at lead 1 changes the AUC by $-0.003$ to $+0.001$ ($p\ge0.62$), and replacing the climatology by a model in the combination does not change it either. Even the operational forecast at the most favourable lead is not resolvably better than the climatology with 529 test months.

\section{External Benchmark}
\label{app:external}

\textbf{Design} (recorded before any fit, \path{audit/prediction_external.txt}; \path{exp53_external_benchmark.py}). California Housing (scikit-learn), 20{,}640 block groups and 8 features; target: median house value above the training median (1.608, in units of \$100{,}000). Clusters: cells of $0.25^\circ$ latitude by $0.25^\circ$ longitude (518 cells, from 1 to 1{,}494 rows); split by cell with seed 2026 into 259 training (7{,}669 rows), 77 validation (2{,}689) and 182 test cells (10{,}282). Models, 5 seeds: logistic regression on standardized features ($C=1$; deterministic), Random Forest (300 trees, minimum leaf 5), XGBoost (depth 6, learning rate 0.05, early stopping on the validation AUC) and a multilayer perceptron $(64,32)$ with early stopping. Primary procedure: CR2 with Bell--McCaffrey degrees of freedom over cells, on 5-seed means. Reference population: the 39 test cells with 10--60 rows (1{,}170 rows); small tests: 1{,}000 draws of 19 of them without replacement (423--735 rows, median 570).

\section{Published QML Comparisons: Screening}
\label{app:literature}

\textbf{Search.} The reanalysis is illustrative and the search was targeted, not systematic. On 21 November 2026 the candidates were collected from three sources: the QML studies already cited in the main text; five web searches, whose queries are listed in \path{exp60_literature_reanalysis.py} (rainfall and weather classification; event classification in high-energy physics; medical diagnosis; fraud detection; chest radiographs), and the papers they returned; and binary-classification benchmarks in particle physics that compare a quantum model with BDT, SVM or neural baselines. No bibliographic database was searched, and studies were not screened in duplicate; the sample is therefore small and weighted towards particle physics, and it is not an estimate of how often published comparisons have a given property.

\textbf{Inclusion.} A study enters \Cref{M-tab:literature} if it compares a quantum and a classical binary classifier on held-out test data and prints, for the same setting, the test size and the test AUC of both. 18 studies were read (arXiv versions): 4 report only regression errors, 9 do not meet the criterion (\Cref{tab:literature_screened}), and 5 enter, with 8 comparisons (best quantum against best classical model in the setting the authors emphasise). Two further candidates are not counted: arXiv 2503.23408 has been withdrawn by its authors and has no full text, and arXiv 2610.17027 reports, according to its abstract, the area under the precision--recall curve, not the ROC AUC that the criterion requires (its full text was not read). The five included studies are cited in their published versions, and the values were transcribed from the arXiv versions. Two studies were checked against their published versions: Heredge et al.\ (test size, the AUCs of Table~1 and the definition of their uncertainty) and Belis et al.\ (test size and all three AUC pairs; the published text does not state the class balance or whether the $\pm$ values are standard deviations or standard errors, and it gives the VQC more precisely as $0.662\pm0.015$). For Grossi et al., the published version confirms the wording (``similar'') and a test set of 1{,}000 records, but its AUC tables could not be extracted; the AUCs are those of the arXiv version. The row uses the counts of Table~I of the arXiv version, 521 genuine and 478 fraudulent test records (999, against 1{,}000 in the text of both versions; the difference does not change the rounded values of \Cref{M-tab:literature}). The split was run ``in five separate trials, preserving the same fraudulent to genuine transaction ratio'', and whether the trials share one test set is not stated; the row treats the AUCs as coming from one test set of 999, which gives the wider interval. Wu et al.\ and Kulkarni et al.\ are transcribed from arXiv; their published versions were not accessed, and only their bibliographic data were checked (Crossref).

\textbf{Quantities.} For each comparison the paired Hanley--McNeil standard error at the pooled test size, with the mean of the two AUCs, the printed class balance (0.5 when it is not printed) and an AUC correlation of 0.75 or 0.5, gives an approximate 95\,\% interval, the smallest equivalence margin the data would accept (the bound of \Cref{tab:tost}), and the MDE of \Cref{tab:hm_mde}; test cases are treated as independent, as in every one of these studies. Four of the studies print per-model uncertainties that can be set against this approximation, for one AUC on one test set. Wu et al.\ give standard deviations of 0.03--0.06 over their ten test sets of 100 events, against a Hanley--McNeil standard error of 0.042; the 0.001 of Heredge et al., a standard deviation divided by $\sqrt{10}$, implies 0.003 per test set of 10{,}000, against 0.004; Belis et al.\ give $\pm0.01$--0.02 over five test sets of 720 and Grossi et al.\ $\pm0.01$ over their trials, against 0.020 and 0.013, but neither states whether these are standard deviations or standard errors. The approximation is compatible with the spread the studies print: within the printed range (Wu et al.) or slightly above it (Heredge et al.) where the definition is known; this is a check of plausibility, not a confirmation. Two-decimal AUCs make a difference uncertain by up to $\pm0.01$, which adds to the smallest margins of those rows: in the worst case the six claims of similarity are compatible only with margins of $\pm0.020$ to $\pm0.046$. In Heredge et al.\ the ten test sets are drawn at random from one pool, so the pooled size of 100{,}000 overstates the independent information; with one test set of 10{,}000 the interval still excludes zero. The pooled size matters most for Wu et al., whose ten test sets of 100 events the authors describe as independent datasets without detailing how they were built: with one test set of 100 the ttH interval would include zero. The row therefore uses the pooled size of 1{,}000, as the authors' description supports, and does not carry the note of Heredge et al. \Cref{tab:literature_screened} lists the studies that were read but could not be used. One of them prints a test size without a classical AUC for that setting: the VQC of Terashi et al.\ (2020; arXiv 2002.09935), tested on 40 events (20 per class) with AUC 0.555, has an MDE of 0.18 for a paired difference. The four weather and climate studies of the main text (da Silva et al., Pastori et al., Fellner et al., Marchisio et al.) compare regression errors, for which an interval needs per-case predictions.

\section{Pre-Analysis Plan Template}
\label{app:pap}

The analyses of this paper were not pre-registered; only the predictions for the second panel and the external benchmark were recorded in advance (\path{audit/prediction_*.txt}). The template below fixes before any test prediction is seen the decisions that the protocol (\Cref{M-tab:protocol}) treats as substantive. It is also in the repository (\path{docs/pre_analysis_plan_template.md}), to be committed with a timestamp that the author does not control, such as a Zenodo or OSF deposit.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Estimand.} Metric; single run or mean over $S$ seeds; population of test cases (e.g.\ new years at the same sites); what is conditioned on (training data, fitted models).
\item \textbf{Statements and their interval.} For each comparison, whether it is about the fitted models (decided by the primary cluster interval) or about a procedure or architecture (decided only if an interval that resamples seeds and clusters also excludes zero).
\item \textbf{Clusters and primary procedure.} The cluster unit, the number of clusters, the primary procedure and its reference distribution; the sensitivity analyses (e.g.\ clusters of consecutive periods, wild cluster bootstrap); the cluster/iid ratio below which the standard error is floored at the iid one, or the mechanism expected to produce low ratios.
\item \textbf{Calibration.} The simulation that will check the primary procedure on the test design: mechanisms, true differences, number of replications, and the coverage band that counts as calibrated.
\item \textbf{Families and multiplicity.} The primary comparisons, grouped in families, and the correction (e.g.\ Holm); everything else declared secondary.
\item \textbf{Resolution.} The MDE of each primary comparison, computed from the test size, the prevalence and an assumed AUC and correlation (\Cref{tab:hm_mde}) and multiplied by the square root of the design effect of the clusters, $\sqrt{1+(m-1)\rho}$ (the design effect multiplies the variance, not the standard error; critical values kept, under that approximation), with its expected uncertainty.
\item \textbf{Equivalence.} The smallest difference of interest and the equivalence margin, chosen by relevance and independently of the MDE.
\item \textbf{References and controls.} The base-rate reference, any operational reference, and, for quantum models, the classical controls at the circuit interface and the planted signals with their strengths and null.
\item \textbf{Robustness rules.} Which checks (e.g.\ omitted clusters, consecutive-period clusters, causal refits) turn a resolved difference into an exploratory one.
\item \textbf{Predictions.} The resolution and the verdicts expected, recorded with the plan.
\end{enumerate}
