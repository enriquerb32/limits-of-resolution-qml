\section{Data, Labels and Features}
\label{app:pipeline}

\subsection{Monthly Panel and Labels}

The daily files of each station (precipitation; maximum and minimum pressure; maximum and minimum temperature; sunshine duration; mean wind speed and maximum gust) are read separately and placed on a full daily calendar from the first to the last date found in any of them (\path{code/etl_v2.py}). Precipitation is taken from its own file only: a trace (``Ip'') counts as 0\,mm, and an empty or unparseable entry is missing. At the daily level, covariates stay missing where they are missing; no value is carried forward or backward. \Cref{tab:etl} lists the spans and the shares of missing days.

Monthly aggregation (\path{code/local_experiments/dataset_v2.py}) produces, per station and calendar month: the \emph{label total}, the sum of daily precipitation, defined when at most 3 days of the month are missing; the \emph{precipitation lag}, the sum over observed days rescaled to the month length, defined with at least 10 observed days; and the covariates, means over observed days (sunshine and ET$_0$ sums as means times the month length), defined with at least 10 observed days, with at most two consecutive missing months interpolated linearly within the station (in a longer gap, its first two months; \Cref{sec:lookahead}). ET$_0$ follows FAO-56 Penman--Monteith with $u_2=0.748\,u_{10}$ from the observed mean wind (FAO-56 eq.~47 for a 10\,m anemometer). Every calendar month is kept, and a window with target month $t$ is used only if $t$ has a label total, month $t-1$ has all covariates and the 12 lags $t-12,\dots,t-1$ are defined. Training-partition medians replace undefined cells before the min--max scalers are fitted, so that rows that never enter a window do not stretch the scales. Thresholds ($P_{60}=29.9$\,mm, $P_{90}$) are percentiles of all 2{,}847 labelled training months of the four stations (Alicante 699, Barcelona 809, San Javier 629, Valencia 710), not only of the months that are window targets.

\subsection{Lookahead Audit}
\label{sec:lookahead}

A forecast for month $t$ is issued at the end of month $t-1$, so its inputs may use only data up to that date. Three steps of the monthly pipeline used for the results of the paper do not satisfy this. (i) The interpolation of missing monthly covariates is two-sided: a missing $t-1$ is filled from $t-2$ and $t$, and the first two months of a longer gap from the month after the gap, up to 18 months later. (ii) The antecedent-summer evapotranspiration (\texttt{et0\_verano\_prev}) of a summer with an incomplete June--August record is interpolated towards the next complete summer and back-filled. (iii) A missing one-month evapotranspiration lag is replaced by the station mean over the full record, test years included. The chronological split does not prevent any of these.

\textbf{Truncation test} (\path{exp47_truncation_audit.py}). For each test target month $t$ the monthly panel was rebuilt from the daily file cut at the end of $t-1$, with the transformations fitted on the training partition (anomaly statistics, medians, scalers and $P_{60}$, all from 1938--1996 and therefore unaffected by the cut); the label of $t$ is the outcome and comes from the full record. For all 529 test windows the 35 inputs and the eligibility of the window are identical to those of the full pipeline, and no window becomes eligible only after the cut. The reason is that the gaps that the interpolation fills lie before the test period or at stations whose records stop: 54 station-months receive an interpolated covariate; 26 of them are the last antecedent month of an eligible window (24 training, 2 validation windows) and none of a test window. The incomplete summers after 2011 (San Javier) and 2012 (Valencia) have no later complete summer to interpolate from. The daily file does not record when each value became available, so this is a retrospective check of lookahead in the processing, not a reconstruction of what could have been issued in real time.

\textbf{Causal pipeline.} With \texttt{V2\_CAUSAL=1}, a missing covariate is carried forward from the last observed month for at most two months, \texttt{et0\_verano\_prev} is the last complete summer (before the first one, the station's training median), and a missing evapotranspiration lag takes the station's training mean. The same truncation test gives no change for any test window. The causal panel has 533 test windows instead of 529, because carrying forward also fills the last months before the covariate records of Valencia and San Javier stop. \Cref{tab:lookahead} compares models retrained on the two panels on the 529 common test windows: the differences are an order of magnitude below the standard error of one AUC (0.024) and below every minimum detectable difference between complete models.

\textbf{Label completeness.} The label rule admits up to 3 missing days, and with missing days the observed total is a lower bound of the true one, so that a fixed threshold can only produce false negatives. In this panel all 529 test targets, all 528 labelled validation months and all 2{,}847 labelled training months that define $P_{60}$ have complete records; one labelled month of the test period has missing days but is not a window target. A sensitivity analysis restricted to complete months therefore reproduces the reported AUCs and $P_{60}$ exactly.

\subsection{Engineered Features}
\label{sec:glossary}

Each window has 35 covariates: 12 precipitation lags, 17 physical and synoptic indicators of the last antecedent month (\Cref{tab:glossary}), 2 cyclic terms of the target month and 4 station indicators. Anomalies and scaling statistics are computed from the training years (1938--1996) only. The covariates are candidate predictors, not physical state variables of a numerical weather prediction model.

\subsection{Tuning of the Tree Ensembles}
\label{app:hyperparams}

Both tree ensembles were tuned by the same declared search (\path{exp40_v2_tune.py}): Optuna, TPE sampler with seed 0, 50 trials each, objective the validation AUC of a model fitted on the training rows with model seed 0. Random Forest: 100--500 trees (step 50), depth 3--16, minimum split 2--20, minimum leaf 1--10, $\sqrt{p}$, $\log_2 p$ or half of the features. XGBoost: learning rate 0.01--0.3 (log), depth 2--8, minimum child weight 1--20 (log), subsample and column subsample 0.5--1, $\lambda$ $10^{-3}$--10 (log), $\gamma$ 0--5, early stopping on the validation AUC. The validation partition is used both for early stopping and for the search; the test partition is not touched. With the selected configurations (\Cref{tab:core_spec}) the 15-seed test AUCs are 0.6784 (SD 0.0011) for Random Forest and 0.6684 (0.0081) for XGBoost, against 0.6773 (0.0037; 300 trees of depth 8) and 0.6531 (0.0054; 500 trees of depth 4, learning rate 0.05, no early stopping) for the untuned configurations of the earlier version; the differences, $+0.001$ (DeLong $p=0.69$) and $+0.015$ ($p=0.27$), are not resolved. In the search the validation AUC of XGBoost peaked after very few boosting rounds. The neural models were not tuned automatically; their configuration is that of the earlier version.

\subsection{Few-Cluster Corrections and Descriptive Bounds}
\label{app:fewclusters}

\textbf{Calibration of the primary procedure} (\path{exp48_primary_calibration.py}, 600 replications per cell, Monte Carlo standard error of a coverage of 0.95 about 0.009). Rows are the 529 station-months of the test set with their stations and years, or variants with 10 or 38 years of the same station patterns and with two or four stations in every year. Two binormal scorers ($\rho=0.85$, AUC 0.66 and $0.66+\Delta$, $\Delta\in\{0,0.02\}$) receive year effects on the prevalence logit and on a shared score shift ($\tau=0.3$), scorer-specific year shifts ($\tau_m=0.15$), scorer-specific year$\times$station effects with opposite loadings at Alicante/San Javier and Barcelona/Valencia ($\tau_c=0.25$), a prevalence dipole between the two pairs of stations combined with a scorer that uses the station base rate, and AR(1) dependence between consecutive years (coefficient 0.5). The truth is the difference for new independent years, analytic where no scorer uses the base rate (\Cref{M-app:noise_budget}) and computed on 300 independent copies of the design otherwise. \Cref{tab:primary_cal} summarizes the coverage of the 95\,\% intervals.

\subsection{Frequency Spectrum and Angles of the Trained Circuits}
\label{app:spectrum}

For each of the 15 trained circuits of \texttt{LSTM+QNN}, the exact readouts $\langle Z_j\rangle$ were evaluated on the angles produced for the training and test rows (\path{exp34_circuit_spectrum.py}, part A). For the spectrum, each angle was swept over a full period $[0,2\pi)$ with the other three at the values of a test row, so the shares below describe the function on the whole torus; the least-squares fits that follow use only the angles the model actually visits (roughly 0.4--2.6\,rad, less than half a period), on which higher frequencies are well approximated by degree-1 terms. The two statements are therefore compatible: 24\,\% of the variance at frequency 2 over the full period, and $R^2=0.984$ for the degree-1 tensor basis on the visited range. Split by the per-coordinate frequency of its Fourier components, the variance of the readout over the full period is: 68.2\,\%, 24.2\,\%, 6.7\,\%, 0.9\,\% of the variance lies at frequencies 1, 2, 3 and 4 (circuits with random weights: 59.6\,\%, 29.1\,\%, 9.9\,\%, 1.4\,\%). Least-squares fits on the training angles, evaluated on the test angles, give $R^2$ of 0.984 (degree-1 tensor basis, 81 functions; range 0.95--1.00), 0.987 (degree-2 tensor basis, 625 functions; 0.88--1.00), 0.850 (separable degree 2) and 0.854 (separable degree 4). Fitting the degree-1 tensor basis to circuits with random weights on the same angles gives 0.974 (0.94--1.00): the near-linearity per coordinate is a property of this encoding on the visited angles. For the median seed, the 1st, 50th and 99th percentiles of the test angles of the four qubits are 0.65, 0.37, 1.01 and 0.58; 1.72, 1.40, 1.86 and 1.45; and 2.58, 2.48, 2.62 and 2.29 rad; between 0 and 14\,\% of the test angles of a seed lie within $\pi/18$ of 0 or $\pi$.

\subsection{Emulation and Substitution on Common Angles}
\label{app:emulation}

\path{exp52_emulation_substitution.py} uses the 15 trained \texttt{LSTM+QNN} models of \path{exp31_shot_noise.py} (\path{checkpoints/exp31/}; their test probabilities reproduce the archived ones to $10^{-6}$; mean AUC 0.660 against 0.659 for the core fits). \emph{Emulation}: the four readouts are evaluated on the grid $q_j\in\{2\pi m/9\}$, $m=0,\dots,8$, and a four-dimensional discrete Fourier transform gives the $9^4=6{,}561$ coefficients of each readout; this is exact because the two re-uploading layers bound the frequencies at 4 per angle. Evaluated on the test angles and passed through the trained LayerNorm and output layer, the reconstruction differs from the circuit by at most $2.6\times10^{-14}$ in the logits, and the AUC is identical for every seed. Keeping only frequencies $|k_j|\le1$ (or $\le2$) per angle loses 0.058 (0.011) AUC on average, with a maximum of 0.215 (0.070), and changes the readouts by up to 0.95 (0.64) on uniform angles. \emph{Substitution}: for each seed, the LSTM and projection are frozen in evaluation mode and the angles of the training, validation and test rows computed once; on them the circuit (new random weights), Linear-4D, separable Fourier-2 and the 81- and 625-function tensor maps, each followed by LayerNorm and a linear output, are trained with the loop of the paper (AdamW $5\times10^{-3}$, batch 16, at most 25 epochs, patience 6) and the seed of the frozen model. Every head of a seed shares its extractor, so the years$\times$seeds bootstrap draws seeds jointly.

\subsection{Positive Control with a Planted Circuit Signal}
\label{app:teacher}

\path{exp38_teacher_control.py} keeps the real inputs, splits and scalers. Teacher angles are $q^\ast=\pi\,\sigma(1.5\,ZP)$, with $Z$ the inputs standardized with training statistics and $P$ a fixed $35\times4$ Gaussian projection; the teacher score is $s=w^\top\langle Z\rangle(q^\ast;W^\ast)$ for the circuit of \texttt{LSTM+QNN} with fixed random weights $W^\ast$ and readout $w$. Among 20 candidate teachers, number 12 was kept because the largest share of its score variance (89\,\%) lies outside a separable degree-4 Fourier basis. Labels are $y=\mathbb 1[s+e>$ training 60th percentile$]$ with Gaussian noise $e$ whose scale (0.82 times the training SD of $s$) gives an oracle AUC of 0.846 on the test rows (test prevalence 0.389). In the \emph{interface} mode every head receives $q^\ast$ directly and consists of its map, LayerNorm and a linear output; in the \emph{end-to-end} mode the complete models of the paper are trained on the raw inputs and must also learn the projection. Five seeds per model, same training loop. The teacher score of this control has no LayerNorm, whereas every student head standardizes its four readouts before the linear output, so teacher and student are not of exactly the same form.

\textbf{Repeated control} (\path{exp50_teacher_grid.py}, interface mode). The teacher score is $s=w^\top\mathrm{LN}(\langle Z\rangle(q^\ast;W^\ast))$, with the same LayerNorm (without affine parameters) as the students; with this score candidate 0 has the largest non-separable share (0.83) and is the selected teacher. Configurations: candidates 0--3 (non-separable shares 0.83, 0.68, 0.57 and 0.80), none of them selected for its result, at oracle test AUCs of 0.70 and 0.85, and a second noise draw for candidate 0 (10 configurations); and two null configurations in which the labels are thresholded noise. Heads: trained circuit, Linear-4D, separable Fourier-2 and the 81-function tensor basis, 5 seeds each. For each contrast the paired difference of 5-seed mean AUCs is tested with CR2 and Bell--McCaffrey degrees of freedom over calendar years. The trained circuit is above Linear-4D in 6 of 10 configurations (never below), never above Fourier-2 (below it in 2), and below the tensor basis in all 10; under the two nulls no contrast excludes zero. Mean AUCs range from 0.54 to 0.75 for the circuit, 0.56 to 0.76 for Fourier-2 and 0.60 to 0.79 for the tensor basis (\path{local_exp50_teacher_grid.json}).

\subsection{Finite-Shot Inference}
\label{app:shots}

\path{exp31_shot_noise.py} refits the 15 seeds of \texttt{LSTM+QNN} (mean exact AUC 0.6599, against 0.6587 for the archived fits) and, for each seed and shot budget, samples the 16-outcome distribution of the circuit jointly 50 times, estimating each $\langle Z_j\rangle$ from the samples (\Cref{tab:shots}). With parameter-shift gradients, each training row and step would need 81 circuit evaluations: one forward pass, 48 for the 24 weights and 32 for the 16 encoding-gate occurrences through which the classical layers are trained; training on hardware was not simulated. The refit also measures re-execution: neural training on CPU is not bit-reproducible across software versions and thread settings, and within the pinned environment refitting the 15 seeds changed their mean AUC by 0.001, a third of the uncertainty of a 15-seed mean and an order of magnitude below the MDE.

\textbf{Design} (recorded before any fit, \path{audit/prediction_external.txt}; \path{exp53_external_benchmark.py}). California Housing (scikit-learn), 20{,}640 block groups and 8 features; target: median house value above the training median (1.608, in units of \$100{,}000). Clusters: cells of $0.25^\circ$ latitude by $0.25^\circ$ longitude (518 cells, from 1 to 1{,}494 rows); split by cell with seed 2026 into 259 training (7{,}669 rows), 77 validation (2{,}689) and 182 test cells (10{,}282). Reference population: the 39 test cells with 10--60 rows (1{,}170 rows); small tests: 1{,}000 draws of 19 of them without replacement (423--735 rows, median 570).

\textbf{Recorded predictions.} P1 (Random Forest against logistic regression resolved in the reference, above the margin) failed: the difference is $+0.024$, but the 39 reference cells give a CR2 interval of $[-0.022,+0.071]$; the reference population was too small for the spatial dependence, a design error that the analysis keeps rather than corrects after the fact. P2 (power of CR2 for that contrast at least 0.80 and wrong signs at most 2.5\,\%) failed on power (0.036); the wrong-sign part is not informative, because the sign of the reference is not determined for two of the three contrasts (XGBoost and the perceptron against Random Forest change sign between the 39 reference cells and all 182 test cells). P3 (CR2 coverage 0.93--0.97) failed: coverage is 0.941--0.998, conservative; most of the excess is the finite-population effect of drawing 19 of 39 cells, and with 19 of all 182 cells the coverage is 0.960--0.978 (post hoc). P4 held: the contrast within the margin is resolved by CR2 in 0.2\,\% of draws, and DeLong resolves every contrast more often than CR2. P5 held at the edge: the MDE predicted from the reference by $N^{-1/2}$ scaling is 1.37--1.40 times the empirical one ($2.80\times$ the standard deviation of the small-test estimates); a factor close to $1/\sqrt{1-19/39}=1.40$ is expected because the small tests draw half of a finite set of 39 cells, so the agreement says less about the scaling than the number suggests. The two reference populations also disagree: XGBoost is above Random Forest on the reference cells ($+0.028$) and not on all test cells ($-0.002$), a reminder that the reference is itself one clustered sample. Not done: a prospective evaluation, which needs data that do not exist yet.
